1. Who we are

Homeward Paws is a mobile app and website that helps people in South Africa share information about missing, found and sighted animals so they can be reunited safely. Homeward Paws is operated by Digitack (Pty) Ltd, South Africa ("we", "us").

For the purposes of the Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 (POPIA), Digitack (Pty) Ltd is the responsible party for the personal information described in this policy. Our Information Officer is [INFORMATION OFFICER NAME], who can be reached at info@digitack.co.za.

This policy applies to the Homeward Paws Android app, this website and any related support channels.

2. The short version

  • We collect what is needed to run your account and publish the reports you choose to share.
  • Your home address, private holding locations, microchip and tag numbers and ownership evidence are never shown publicly.
  • Your phone number and WhatsApp appear on a report only if you switch them on for that report.
  • We do not sell your personal information.
  • The app shows ads through Google AdMob, which uses your device's advertising ID. You can reset it or opt out of personalised ads in Android settings.
  • You can delete your account in the app or from this website.

4. How we use it

  • To create and secure your account and verify your email address.
  • To publish the reports, sightings and updates you choose to share, and keep a history of each case.
  • To show you reports near your home area or a location you choose.
  • To suggest possible matches between reports, using details such as animal type, colours, size, place and date. People, not the app, confirm a match.
  • To send you notifications about cases you own or follow and, if you choose, new reports near you.
  • To let people contact you about a report in the ways you have allowed.
  • To review ownership claims, which only you and the relevant case participants can see.
  • To moderate content, prevent fraud and abuse, enforce our terms and keep people and animals safe.
  • To provide support, fix problems and improve the app.
  • To show ads that keep the app free.
  • To meet our legal obligations.

Under POPIA we process personal information because it is necessary to provide the service you asked for, because you have consented (for example to location access or optional notifications), because we have a legitimate interest in keeping the service safe, or because the law requires it. Where we rely on consent, you can withdraw it at any time.

5. What other people can see

Homeward Paws exists so that reports can be seen by people who might help. When you publish a report, the following is visible to anyone using the app or our website, including people without an account, and can be shared as a link:

  • The animal's photos, description and safety notes.
  • The general area where the animal was last seen or found: the suburb, town and province by default. You can choose to add a street name or a public landmark (for example a park or shopping centre). We never publish exact map coordinates or house numbers, and the app rejects house numbers in this field.
  • For found reports, a general area only. Where the animal is being kept stays private.
  • Your display name or first name, or "anonymous finder" on found reports if you choose.
  • Your phone number and WhatsApp, only if you switch them on for that report.
  • Public case updates and the report's status, such as reunited or closed.

Sightings you send on someone's missing report are private. They go only to the person who reported the animal missing (and our administrators if a safety review is needed), never to the public. A sighting contains the time and area, what you saw, an optional photo, your exact location only if you choose to share it from your device, and your phone number only if you switch that on for the sighting.

Our website's Animals near you page shows the same published missing reports. It doesn't show the reporter's name or phone number. If you tap Use my location, your browser works out your province on your own device; your location is never sent to us or stored.

Please don't put private details in a report's free-text fields or photos: no house numbers, full addresses, phone numbers you haven't chosen to show, or pictures that reveal where you live. Anything you type or upload in those places is public once the report is published.

Always private: your home address and map location, private holding addresses, full microchip and tag numbers, ownership evidence, draft reports, your email address, and your phone number unless you enable it.

7. Advertising

The app is free and shows static banner ads provided by Google AdMob. Ads never appear on the screens you use to report an animal. To show and measure ads, Google may collect your device's advertising ID, IP address, device information and interactions with ads, and may use cookies or similar technologies. Google's use of this information is governed by Google's privacy policy for partner apps.

You can reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of personalised ads, in your Android settings under Google, then Ads, or Privacy, then Ads, depending on your device.

10. How we protect it

  • All information is encrypted in transit between the app and our servers.
  • Database access rules check every request, so members cannot read each other's private information.
  • Ownership evidence is kept in private storage and opened only through short-lived, signed links for authorised people.
  • Administrative access is limited to authorised staff, and administrative actions are logged.
  • Secret server keys are never included in the app.

No system is perfectly secure. If a security compromise affects your personal information, we will notify you and the Information Regulator as POPIA requires.

12. Deleting your account and data

You can delete your Homeward Paws account at any time, in the app under Profile, then Delete account, or by following the steps on our account deletion page. You do not need the app installed to make a request.

When you delete your account we:

  • Delete your account, profile, contact details, home area, profile photo and push tokens.
  • Delete your animal profiles, their photos and private identification details.
  • Delete your drafts, follows, notification settings and ownership evidence.
  • Close your active reports and stop their alerts.
  • Remove your name, contact details, photos and exact locations from your published reports and contributions. A minimal anonymous record of each case (animal type, general area, dates and outcome) may remain so that linked cases and other people's histories stay accurate.

We complete deletion within 30 days of confirming your request. Copies in backups are overwritten within a further 30 days. Moderation records may be kept as described in How long we keep it. We also ask our service providers to delete information they processed for us. Information held by Google for advertising is controlled through your Google and Android settings.

You can also delete individual items without deleting your account: delete an animal profile, a draft or a photo in the app, or ask us to remove specific information by emailing info@digitack.co.za.

13. Your rights

Under POPIA you have the right to:

  • Ask whether we hold personal information about you and request a copy of it.
  • Ask us to correct, update or delete it.
  • Object to processing on reasonable grounds, and object to direct marketing.
  • Withdraw consent where we rely on it.
  • Lodge a complaint with the Information Regulator of South Africa at inforegulator.org.za.

To use these rights, email info@digitack.co.za from the address linked to your account. We may need to confirm your identity first, and we will respond within 30 days.

14. Children

Homeward Paws accounts are for people aged 18 and older. Younger people may browse public reports, and should ask a parent or guardian to post or contact anyone for them. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has created an account, contact us and we will delete it. Our child safety standards explain how we deal with any content that endangers children.
