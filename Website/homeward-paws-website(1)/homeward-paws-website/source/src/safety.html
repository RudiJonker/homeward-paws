1. If your animal is missing

  • Search your home and garden first: sheds, garages, under decks and inside cars. Frightened animals often hide close by.
  • Publish a missing report as soon as you can, with clear photos and the place and time you last saw your animal.
  • Say how people should approach your animal. Many lost animals run if chased, even from people they know.
  • Contact local vets, the SPCA and shelters, and ask them to scan any animal brought in for a microchip.
  • Keep your report up to date. A new confirmed area helps people look in the right place.
  • Never post your microchip number publicly. Keep it in your private animal profile to prove ownership.

2. If you have found an animal

  • Only secure an animal if it is safe for you. If it is aggressive, injured or in traffic, call a vet, the SPCA or animal control.
  • Ask a vet, shelter or SPCA to scan it for a microchip. Most do this free of charge.
  • Publish a found report. Your exact address stays private and the public sees only a general area.
  • Check possible matches with missing reports nearby.
  • Before handing an animal over, ask for proof such as matching photos, vet records or a microchip scan.
  • If the animal is still roaming and you could not secure it, report a sighting instead.

3. If you see an animal loose

  • Note where and when you saw it and which way it went. That is the most useful information.
  • Take a photo only if it is safe. Never photograph while driving, and never chase an animal or follow it onto private property.
  • Log a sighting in the app. It takes about a minute and the owner is told straight away if it is linked to their report.

4. Avoiding scams

Homeward Paws never asks for money, and no genuine finder needs payment to return an animal. Be wary of anyone who:
  • asks for a reward, deposit, "transport fee" or airtime before they will return an animal;
  • claims to have your animal but will not send a recent photo or meet at a vet or shelter;
  • asks for your microchip number, ID number or banking details;
  • pressures you to act immediately or to come alone.

Use Report in the app on any suspicious report or profile, and report extortion to SAPS.

5. Meeting safely

  • Meet at a vet, shelter, SPCA or police station, or in a busy public place during the day.
  • Take someone with you and tell another person where you are going.
  • Do not invite strangers to your home or go to theirs.
  • Do not send children to collect or hand over an animal.
  • Once the animal is safely home, mark the case as reunited so alerts stop.

6. Emergencies

Homeward Paws is not an emergency service.

  • Injured or sick animal: your nearest vet or the local SPCA.
  • Suspected theft or a crime: SAPS on 10111.
  • A child in danger: SAPS on 10111, or Childline on 116.
